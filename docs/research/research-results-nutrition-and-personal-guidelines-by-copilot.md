Comprehensive Nutrition Science Report for an International AI Cooking Assistant
Executive Summary

Across major evidence-based dietary patterns and national dietary guidelines, a strong consensus exists: diets emphasizing vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, nuts, and unsaturated fats while limiting highly processed foods, excess sodium, added sugars, and processed meats are consistently associated with better health outcomes. The strongest evidence supports cardiovascular risk reduction, improved blood pressure, lower LDL cholesterol, reduced type 2 diabetes risk, and improved overall diet quality.

For a consumer cooking assistant, the most useful approach is to translate nutrition science into:

Hard constraints (e.g., peanut allergy, strict vegetarianism)
Health targets (e.g., lower LDL, increase fiber)
Preferences (e.g., dislikes mushrooms)

The assistant should support healthy eating and recipe adaptation, but should explicitly avoid individualized medical nutrition therapy.

Medical Nutrition Therapy Red Flags

Refer to physician or registered dietitian when users report:

Chronic Kidney Disease

Requires individualized potassium, phosphorus, protein management.

Diabetes on Medication

Hypoglycemia risk.

Eating Disorders

Requires specialist care.

Severe Food Allergies
Pregnancy Complications
Malabsorption Disorders
Complex GI Disease
Cancer Treatment
Food-Drug Interactions

Examples:

Vitamin K ↔ warfarin
Grapefruit ↔ certain medications

These should trigger referral and caution.

Appropriate Disclaimer

The assistant should state that it:

Supports healthy cooking and meal planning
Does not diagnose or treat disease
Does not replace medical advice
Recommends professional consultation when medical conditions may require individualized nutrition guidance
Regulatory Considerations
EU

Health claims are tightly regulated by EFSA and EU regulations.

US

Structure/function claims differ from disease-treatment claims.

The assistant should avoid implying diagnosis, treatment, cure, or prevention unless accurately reflecting authorized public-health guidance.
