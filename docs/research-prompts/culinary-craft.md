# Research Prompt: culinary craft

**For:** every cc-chef skill that creates or improves a recipe.
**Why research helps:** the main complaint about generic AI recipe ideas is that they are doable but forgettable. The target is everyday food — comfort food, traditional fare, rustic home cooking — made memorable by a deliberate touch, without tipping into experimental cooking. This research should give the skills concrete, evidence-aware principles for that, instead of vague "add flavor" advice.

Paste the prompt below into a deep-research AI tool. Save the response as `docs/research/research-results-culinary-craft-by-<tool>.md`.

```text
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Research prompt: what makes home cooking memorable
─────────────────────────────────────────────
You are a research analyst with expertise in culinary arts, food science, and sensory science. I am building an AI cooking assistant that creates and improves recipes for home cooks. Its recipes must be practical and approachable — everyday dishes, comfort food, traditional and rustic home cooking — but they should have more finesse than typical generic recipes: a well-chosen spice, a finishing touch, a contrast that makes a dish worth remembering. They must NOT become experimental, fusion-for-its-own-sake, or star-chef-level complex. The assistant is used internationally, in many languages and culinary traditions.

Provide a comprehensive, well-structured report using headers to separate these areas:

1. FLAVOR BALANCE
   The principles cooks use to balance a dish: the basic tastes (salty, sour, sweet, bitter, umami), fat, heat/pungency, and aroma. Cover practitioner frameworks (e.g. Samin Nosrat's "Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat") and what sensory science and food chemistry say about them — e.g. how salt and acid change perceived flavor, how umami and kokumi build depth, how fat carries aroma. Distinguish well-evidenced mechanisms from kitchen lore.

2. BUILDING DEPTH
   Techniques that create depth in everyday cooking: flavor bases and aromatics across cuisines (e.g. soffritto, mirepoix, sofrito, the Cajun "holy trinity", tempering/tadka, spice pastes), browning (Maillard reaction, caramelization, fond, deglazing), blooming spices in fat, toasting, reducing, layering seasoning over time. Explain the mechanism behind each and when it is worth the extra step for a home cook.

3. FINISHING TOUCHES AND CONTRAST
   The small additions that lift a dish at the end: fresh herbs, a squeeze of acid, a drizzle of good fat, herb sauces (e.g. gremolata, salsa verde, chimichurri), compound butters, crunchy toppings (toasted nuts, seeds, breadcrumbs), pickled elements. Include sensory research on texture and temperature contrast (e.g. "dynamic contrast") and its effect on liking.

4. FLAVOR PAIRING: EVIDENCE VS. MYTH
   What research actually supports about ingredient pairing — e.g. the "food pairing hypothesis" and the flavor-network study by Ahn et al. (2011), regional differences between cuisines, and critiques of the hypothesis. Also cover practitioner pairing references (e.g. "The Flavor Bible", "The Flavour Thesaurus") and their status as curated experience rather than science.

5. MEMORABLE VS. MERELY COMPETENT
   What distinguishes a dish people remember and ask for again from one that is merely fine — perspectives from chefs, cookbook authors, test kitchens, and research on food liking, memorability, and the role of a single "signature" element. Where does refinement end and "experimental" begin from a home cook's perspective? Include research on food neophobia and familiarity, and on how much novelty people accept in otherwise familiar dishes.

6. RESPECTING TRADITIONAL DISHES
   How to add finesse to traditional and regional dishes without losing what makes them what they are: which elements are core to a dish's identity and which are open to variation. Draw on several culinary traditions (European, Asian, Middle Eastern, African, Latin American), not only one.

7. PRACTICAL REVIEW CHECKLIST
   Synthesize the above into a checklist a cook (or an AI) could apply when reviewing a draft recipe for balance, depth, contrast, and a signature touch. Also list the typical failure modes of generic or AI-generated recipes (e.g. under-seasoning, too many competing ingredients, missing acid, techniques that don't fit the dish, bland "safe" defaults) and how to fix each.

Requirements:
- Use credible, verifiable sources throughout, with in-text citations: peer-reviewed food science and sensory research, culinary textbooks, and well-established cookbook authors and test kitchens. Avoid generic food-blog content.
- Be explicit about which principles are backed by empirical research and which are practitioner experience or tradition.
- Use concrete dish examples from multiple culinary traditions.
- Provide a formatted source list in APA style at the end.
- Respond in Markdown.
─────────────────────────────────────────────
```
